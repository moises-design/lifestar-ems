# Life Star EMS V2 — Creative Direction

Date: 2026-07-24
Branch: `claude/lifestar-seo-foundation`
Status: research and direction only. No production code in this mission.

Working name for the direction: **"Calm Response"** — quiet competence,
documentary humanity, editorial calm.

---

## 1. Executive design vision

Life Star EMS should look like the company a hospital discharge planner, a
school athletic director, and a worried parent would all instinctively trust.
Today the site looks like an energetic local startup; V2 should look like a
regional healthcare institution that happens to be young and modern.

The V2 direction replaces the current dark "space theme" with a light,
editorial, photography-led design: large calm serif headlines, warm off-white
backgrounds, one disciplined brand blue, documentary photographs of real crews
and real moments, generous whitespace, and numbered, chaptered storytelling.
The site should feel closer to a premium healthcare-technology company than to
any ambulance-company template, while staying unmistakably local to the Rio
Grande Valley through photography, light, and place names rather than clichés.

## 2. What is currently working

- Strong conversion scaffolding: persistent mobile call bar, tel: links
  everywhere, a short request form, clear per-service CTAs.
- A clear four-service structure that maps to real queries (dialysis, therapy,
  pediatrics/long-distance, event standby).
- A real brand mark (blue diamond cross) that survives small sizes (now the
  favicon set).
- The new SEO foundation: per-route metadata, robots/sitemap, schema, 404.
- Brand blue `#0B9ED9` is distinctive against the red/white of typical EMS.

## 3. What currently feels dated or generic

- The space theme (star fields, shooting stars, nebulas, rockets, alien emoji)
  reads as playful consumer app, not medical transport; it also costs
  performance and accessibility.
- Dark navy everywhere flattens every section into the same weight; nothing
  breathes, nothing is editorial.
- Emoji used as iconography in navigation, headings, and cards.
- Generic blue icon-card grids (the exact pattern the brief asks to avoid).
- Condensed sports-style typography (Barlow Condensed) says "athletics
  department", not "healthcare partner".
- Trust is asserted (badges, logo walls, a 5.0 rating) rather than shown
  (photography, named people, verifiable specifics). Several of those
  assertions are unverified and must not carry into V2.
- No real photography of Life Star people or vehicles anywhere on the site.

## 5. Additional Mobbin references

1. **Superpower** (healthcare diagnostics) — [Outcomes section](https://mobbin.com/sites/sections/0189e9ce-1d76-47e4-af1e-ba341224bd35).
   Documentary portrait testimonials: soft natural-light photos of real
   members, first name + age, a short quote, and a one-line factual context
   underneath, with an honest third-party rating (`4.6 out of 5, Trustpilot`)
   shown small. **Why it helps Life Star:** this is the exact model for our
   future reviews section: real riders and families, dignified photography,
   verifiable rating source, no stars-theater. It also proves healthcare
   proof can be warm without being saccharine.
2. **Waabi / Aurora** (autonomous trucking / logistics) —
   [Waabi hero](https://mobbin.com/sites/sections/0345226a-541f-4607-89b7-b6e0923cb872),
   [Aurora hero](https://mobbin.com/sites/sections/884f76c3-6c90-4f33-b2e9-10834e483daa).
   Premium fleet photography: golden-hour vehicles in real landscapes with
   giant type set over the image; the vehicle is shown working, not posed on
   a white background. **Why it helps Life Star:** the ambulance-at-sunrise
   photography plan needs exactly this treatment; it shows how a vehicle
   company earns "premium" through cinematic-but-honest imagery instead of
   badges. (Waabi's serif-over-photo hero is also a useful counterpoint to
   Amigo's text-on-paper hero.)
3. **Ease** (healthcare operations) —
   [Patients at the core](https://mobbin.com/sites/sections/6695790f-7142-4e99-86a9-2632963b795d).
   Serif headline in a deep calm green, one short paragraph, then a pairing
   of flat geometric brand panel + warm interior photograph of two people
   talking. **Why it helps Life Star:** shows "clinical without cold" at
   small scale: one accent color, one human photo, one quiet graphic. This is
   the template energy for our service-page sub-sections.
4. **Hims** (consumer health) — [404 page](https://mobbin.com/sites/sections/d1277569-a3eb-49b0-b201-7a2f6b59b30b).
   A 404 that stays on-brand: warm portrait, a small joke, and a two-column
   sitemap of useful links. **Why it helps Life Star:** our V1.5 404 page is
   functional; V2 can make it warm and useful in the same way (photo + link
   columns), which matters for a site families reach under stress.

Rejected while searching (examples of what not to follow): Robot.com and
Freshman heroes (flashy, promotional), DICE gradient hero (event-brand
energy, wrong tone), generic SaaS icon grids (Webflow features capture).

## 6. What to borrow conceptually (not copy)

- Serif editorial display + quiet sans support as the core typographic idea.
- One accent color doing all the work; for us that accent is Life Star blue,
  not Amigo's terracotta.
- Numbered chapter labels in small caps/mono as a navigation-of-the-page
  device (`01 Dialysis`, `02 Pediatric therapy`, ...).
- One dark "operations" chapter on an otherwise light page (our navy heritage
  becomes a single deliberate dark band instead of the whole site).
- Photo collages with small numbered caption chips for crew/operations
  storytelling.
- Trust = named specifics presented soberly; stat columns only when the
  numbers are real and verified.
- Footer as a confident full-bleed brand block with micro-caps link columns.
- Superpower's documentary portrait-testimonial format (future, once real
  reviews are verified).
- Waabi/Aurora's working-vehicle golden-hour photography language.

## 7. What must not be copied

- Amigo's exact typefaces, headline phrasing, layout dimensions, component
  designs, footer artwork, red/terracotta palette, or the "Digital Residency"
  concept and naming.
- Waabi/Aurora imagery or any reference photography itself.
- Superpower's copy, portrait photos, or Trustpilot claim (we have no
  verified rating yet; the section ships only as a placeholder until we do).
- No reference content, illustrations, source code, or assets of any kind
  enter the repo. References inform proportions, tone, and discipline only.

## 8. Proposed visual direction

- **Base**: warm paper off-white; ink is a deep blue-black (evolved from the
  existing navy, used as text and for one dark chapter + footer).
- **Accent**: Life Star blue `#0B9ED9` strictly for actions, links, key
  moments; a darker accessible blue for text-level links; a faint sky tint
  for panels. Optional whisper of sunrise amber inside photography only.
- **Type**: editorial serif display (free: Source Serif 4) + humanist sans
  body (free: Inter). Detailed scale in V2-DESIGN-SYSTEM.md.
- **Imagery**: documentary photography of crews, patients (with dignity and
  consent), vehicles at golden hour, RGV light and places. AI mock
  photography as clearly-labeled temporary placeholders (see
  V2-PHOTOGRAPHY-PLAN.md).
- **Graphic devices**: hairline rules, numbered micro-caps labels, the
  diamond mark used sparingly (never as wallpaper), a redrawn quiet coverage
  map on light background.
- **Explicitly out**: star fields, nebulas, rockets, emoji as UI, red
  emergency theatrics, sirens, cross patterns, icon-card grids, fake stats.

## 9. Proposed emotional tone

Calm, unhurried, exact. The reader should feel: "these people are on time,
they have done this a thousand times, and they will be kind to my mother."
Copy voice: plain sentences, second person, no exclamation points, no
em dashes, no jargon, Spanish welcomed (bilingual line pending owner
confirmation of staffing reality).

## 10. Brand differentiation

Typical RGV medical-transport and EMS sites are red/white/blue templates with
stock ambulances, badge walls, and ALL-CAPS urgency. V2 differentiates by
looking like the healthcare-technology tier (Amigo, Ease) while staying human
and local: real faces, real Valley light, one blue, serif calm. The premium
look is itself the trust argument for hospitals, schools, and municipalities.

## 11. Desktop direction

Chaptered single-column storytelling on a 12-column grid: text columns hold a
readable measure (~65ch) on the left rail; media balances right or goes
full-bleed between chapters. Hero is editorial (statement + documentary
photo), not a slider. Sticky quiet header that gains a hairline + blur on
scroll. Details in V2-DESIGN-SYSTEM.md and V2-PAGE-ARCHITECTURE.md.

## 12. Mobile direction

Mobile is the primary reading experience for families. Single column,
generous type (min 17px body), chapters compress to photo-then-text, the
numbered index becomes a horizontal scroll rail, and the existing persistent
bottom call/request bar is retained and restyled (it is the best conversion
feature of V1). Menus: full-screen sheet with large tap targets, no emoji.
Since Mobbin lacks Amigo mobile-nav evidence, mobile nav follows our own
accessibility requirements rather than any reference.

## 13. Accessibility considerations

- WCAG 2.2 AA contrast throughout; brand blue `#0B9ED9` fails AA for body
  text on white, so text-level links use the darker accessible blue
  (validated in V2-DESIGN-SYSTEM.md).
- All decorative imagery `aria-hidden`; meaningful photos get real alt text.
- Full `prefers-reduced-motion` support; no autoplaying video with sound; no
  parallax.
- Form labels programmatically associated; visible focus states; skip link;
  keyboard-complete navigation (fixes audit findings A11y 1-8).
- Type scale respects user zoom (rem-based); tap targets 48px+.

## 14. SEO preservation requirements (hard constraints for every V2 mission)

- `src/seo/routeMeta.js`, `src/components/Seo.jsx`, `public/robots.txt`,
  `public/sitemap.xml`, `public/site.webmanifest`, the favicon set, the
  JSON-LD block, and the 404 noindex behavior must survive every redesign
  mission unchanged unless a mission explicitly improves them.
- Route paths do not change. Any new routes are additive and enter the
  sitemap in the same mission.
- Every page keeps exactly one `h1` and a logical heading order.
- The verified dispatch number (956) 660-6543 is the only phone number.
- No unverified claims re-enter the UI (ratings, partners, insurance,
  licensing, hours, 24/7, response times) pending the fact sheet.
- Mission 10 of the implementation plan re-verifies all of this before any
  production consideration.

## 15. Risks and open questions

- **Photography is the direction's oxygen.** Without at least temporary
  documentary images, the editorial layout collapses into empty type. The AI
  placeholder plan de-risks this but real photography must be scheduled.
- **Owner voice**: does the owner want the crew/company visible (names,
  faces)? The direction assumes yes; if not, the trust chapters lean on
  process and vehicles instead.
- **Verified proof is thin today**: no confirmed rating, partners, or
  testimonials. V2 designs the slots but ships them empty or hidden until
  facts clear; the page must read complete without them.
- **Brand blue discipline** will be culturally hard after a blue-everything
  site; the design system enforces where blue may appear.
- **Serif choice risk**: Source Serif 4 must be tested against the wordmark;
  if it fights the logo's geometry, fall back to the secondary option in the
  design system.
- **Performance**: photography-led design needs the image pipeline (AVIF/WebP,
  responsive sizes, lazy loading) delivered in the same missions, not after.
- Open question: should Spanish-language V2 land as `/es/` in this redesign
  cycle or immediately after? (Owner decision; affects Mission 7 scope.)
